# AI: lessons from NR2003

NASCAR Racing 2003 Season (Papyrus, 2003) is still the reference for oval racing AI. This page sets out
what made it work, how it compares with Draftmaster 3's AI today, and the order to bring the useful parts
across. Phase 1 is built. The other phases are planned.

**Road-course starts (2026-10-13).** A Watkins Glen start stacked the field nose to tail on the left (the racing
line into Turn 1) with the right half of the road empty. Three causes in `AIRacingBehaviour`, measured with the
rolling-start sim `PackRaceSimTests.WatkinsGlenStart` (24 cars double file at the green, 30 s, five rosters):
the follow cap only looked at the *nearest* car in the corridor, so a car diagonally ahead with a sliver of
overlap hid the car square in front (now every car in the corridor caps; superspeedway packs unchanged); the
wide road-course "contact" window scrubbed speed off a car following 5-12 m behind in the same column, which
ratcheted whole columns toward 30 mph (now only cars actually alongside); and the passing side was always the
outside of the next turn - the side the line already uses (now scored on room and on traffic on each side). A
car off the line also no longer merges back across a car alongside. Start sim before/after: contacts 35 -> 5,
hard 28 -> 1.4, cars stacked under 30 mph 14 -> 1, last car's distance in 30 s 588 -> 1135 m. Racing laps
(`WatkinsGlenRacing`, 4 rosters x 4 laps): passes 3 -> 17, contacts 10 -> 30, hard 8 -> 17 - two-wide through
Turn 1 and the bus stop with both cars sliding; HEAD never got alongside. `RacingRegression`: Charlotte and
Martinsville as good or better, Talladega identical. Dead ends measured: sizing the pass offset to clear the
car ahead (to 6 m) and holding the formation column until the line is clear both stacked the start up worse;
closing-speed evasion and a wider side-by-side gap made no difference or worse.
Follow-up the same night, trying to cut the racing-lap contacts without giving the start back (WG racing 4 rosters
x 4 laps | start 5 rosters; baseline 17 passes / 30 contacts / HARD 17 | 4.8 contacts / HARD 1.4): the trailing car
of a squeeze (< 2.6-3.0 m sideways, partly alongside) lifting to tuck in - start 41-59 contacts, racing no better;
judging room alongside from the cars' physical lateral (projected in `ResyncToCar`) instead of the brain's planned
`LateralOnTrack` - start 13.8 contacts, racing 30, fewer passes (the plan moves first and the car follows, so spacing
off the car overshoots); that plus `sidewaysWidth` 4 - start 58; a 3 m score bonus for keeping the committed passing
side - racing 27 contacts / HARD 13 but passes 17 -> 12, start 4.2 / HARD 1.6, within seed noise. None shipped. Any
extra sideways shove in a 24-car start turns into rear-ends behind it, so test start and racing together.

**Why the AI lifted at Daytona:** two separate things. The throttle controller chased the brain's accel-curve
ramp, so it sat at 30-40% throttle even down the straights; and the speed plan took the turns at ~150 mph
(cornering margins on margins) while the car could hold ~190. Flat out now drives to the brain's target, the
plate keeps that under what the banking holds, and the grip the turns get is solved for the plate plus a full
tow and push.

**Still to do:** the remaining hard contacts are a car pulling out of line while being pushed (it loses the tow
and push as it goes, and the pusher clips its rear quarter) - a pusher could lift as its leader commits, or
pull-outs could wait for a run big enough to cover the loss. The pack sim has no collision physics, so whether a
3-4 m/s tap spins a car needs play-testing. Non-plate tracks still never use full throttle (same ramp-chasing
controller; their pace is calibrated around it). Panic bounds; `Awareness`; Phase 6 stats; Phase 7 sliders.

## How NR2003's AI works

Papyrus never published the internals. Everything below comes from what its modding community worked out
from the files the game exposed: car files, `track.ini`, `.lp` line files and the setup sliders.

### 1. Ratings are bands, rolled once per event, and kept per track type
Each AI driver's car file has `[AIParamMean]` and `[AIParamDeviation]` blocks. Every rating is a
mean ± deviation band, not a single value. The ratings cover Qualifying, Consistency, Aggression, Finishing,
and one each for **Short Tracks, Speedways, Superspeedways and Road Courses**. When the event loads, the game
rolls a value inside each band, and the driver keeps that rating for this race only. Two things follow:
- **Specialists.** The running order changes from track to track, so the same cars do not win everywhere.
- **Form.** A narrow band makes a driver predictable from week to week. A wide one gives a journeyman an
  occasional great day and a star an occasional bad one. In the community's words, "the closer the min and
  max, the more consistently that driver performs".

Community findings: Consistency ("the ability to run laps without making mistakes") and Qualifying matter a
great deal. Finishing barely registers. Aggression is "propensity to initiate a pass attempt, regardless of
the outcome". A high Aggression rating does not make up for a slow car.

### 2. A racing line with a speed at every point, plus bounded lanes
Each track ships a set of `.lp` files:
- `race.lp` is the preferred line, with a target speed at every point. It is the only file whose speeds the
  AI uses.
- `minrace.lp` / `maxrace.lp` are the inner and outer edges of the **passing lanes**. To pass, the AI moves
  out "as far as the wheels will remain in contact" with these lines.
- `minpanic.lp` / `maxpanic.lp` are the wider edges used only **under duress or crowding**, for example when
  squeezed three-wide or avoiding a wreck.
- `pit*.lp` are the pit road lines. On the pace laps the two columns follow `minrace` and `maxrace`.

So a pass is never a fixed sideways offset. It is a move into a lane the track author has shaped corner by
corner, and that lane narrows wherever the track does.

### 3. Each track has its own AI settings in `track.ini`
- `ai_drag` / `ai_accel` / `ai_decel` modifiers adjust AI speed separately on the straights, off the corners
  (and at restarts), and into the corners. AI pace is matched to the human per phase of the lap, not with
  one overall multiplier.
- `ai_drafting_distance` (about 1.05 to 1.07) sets how close the AI follows in the draft before pulling out.
  It is the main track-level setting for how much passing happens: lower values mean more passing and more
  contact.
- Global sliders for **AI Strength** (usually 98 to 103%) and **AI Aggression** (80 to 95%). Players found
  that closely matched speeds keep the pack together, and a tight pack is what produces "the big one".

### 4. Behaviour that came out of these rules
- The AI **holds its lane when side by side** through a corner and does not cut down onto a car alongside.
  This is the single biggest reason people trusted racing next to it.
- **Drafting is a decision.** The AI sits in the tow, then pulls out once it has the run.
- **Cautions come from the AI's own mistakes and contact**, and Consistency drives how often that happens.
- **Pit strategy and the pace laps** use the same lines as racing, so the field looks orderly under yellow.

## Where Draftmaster 3 is today

Draftmaster 3's AI is already well developed: `SplineDriver` and `AIRacingBehaviour`, with race phases,
pressure and tyre-wear mistakes, blue flags, local yellows, `DraftAero` tow, payback and draft bonds, a racing
line trained by lap simulation, and pace calibration for each track. The gaps are the ones NR2003 solved with
data:

| NR2003 idea | Draftmaster 3 before this work |
|---|---|
| Ratings per track type | `Driver.ShortTracks/Speedways/Superspeedways/RoadCourses/DirtCourses` were **shown on the driver card and never read by the AI**. Pace came from `Qualifying` alone. |
| Rating bands rolled once per event | Fresh `Random.Range` jitter on every spawn. Practice, qualifying and the race each re-rolled the field. |
| `minrace` / `maxrace` passing lanes | A fixed `overtakeLineOffset = 3 m` either side, regardless of track or corner |
| `minpanic` / `maxpanic` | No separate wider limit when under duress |
| `ai_drafting_distance` per track | One global `followHeadwaySeconds = 0.3`, so a bullring and Talladega were raced the same way |
| `track.ini` AI section | `TrackTuning` already defines `lineSpread` and `cautionProneness` per track type, and **no AI code reads them** |
| Holding a lane side by side | `sidewaysMaxPush` repulsion. Line commitment ends after `commitHoldSeconds`, even mid-corner. |
| Unused stats | `Awareness`, `TyreManagement`, `FuelManagement` and `Adaptability` are also display-only |

## The plan

### Phase 1: event ratings by track type (built)
- `Draftmaster.Sim.AIRatings` (`Assets/Scripts/Sim/AIRatings.cs`) is a pure function, covered by
  `AIRatingsTests`:
  - **Strength mean** = `lerp(Qualifying, track-type aptitude, AptitudeWeight = 0.5)`.
  - **Band half-width** = `lerp(0.10, 0.025, Consistency)`. Consistency sets how wide the band is.
  - **Aggression** gets its own band of ±0.05. It reflects temperament, so it varies less than form.
- `AIDriverBinding.Apply` rolls those bands **once per event**. The rolls are cached per driver id until
  `TrackSelection.CurrentId` changes, so Friday practice and Sunday's race share them. The rolled strength
  replaces raw `Qualifying` as the input to the existing `paceMultiplier` (0.93 to 1.04) and
  `cornerCommitment` ranges. Those ranges are unchanged, so `AIPaceCalibration` stays valid.
- The small per-session jitter on pace and corner commitment is still there. It acts as day-to-day noise.
- **Check in Play Mode:** run a Single Race at a road course and at a superspeedway with the same field. The
  finishing order should shift towards each track type's specialists. Run practice, qualifying and the race
  at one track. A driver's pace should stay the same across all three.

### Phase 2: per-track AI settings (the `track.ini` section)
Extend `TrackTuningData` with NR2003's settings and connect the fields that already exist:
- `draftFollowScale` (the `ai_drafting_distance` equivalent) scales `followHeadwaySeconds` and the
  pass-initiation range. Superspeedways run nose to tail in a pack. Short tracks run close enough to bump.
  Road courses leave a gap.
- `lineSpread` scales `overtakeLineOffset` and `sidewaysMaxPush`.
- `cautionProneness` scales `mistakeProbabilityPerSecond`.
- `aiStraightScale` / `aiEntryScale` / `aiExitScale` (the `ai_drag/decel/accel` equivalents) multiply the
  SplineDriver speed profile by corner phase. `AIPaceCalibration` can then fit each phase, not just one
  overall pace.
- Read once in `AIDriverBinding.Apply` from `TrackProfile.Current`. Add EditMode tests in `TrackTuning`.

### Phase 3: lanes instead of offsets (`minrace` / `maxrace` / panic)
- Each track already has an authored leftmost and rightmost line in its `SegmentRacingLine`, and
  `SplineDriver.GetLateralBounds` exposes them. They map directly onto `minrace.lp` and `maxrace.lp`.
- A pass targets the **lane** on the chosen side, i.e. the leftmost or rightmost line at that distance, not
  `±3 m`. The pass then follows the shape of each corner and narrows where the track narrows.
- Add **panic bounds**: the painted edge minus half a car. These are used only when side by side with a car
  on the other side, or when avoiding a wreck.
- `lineSpread` (Phase 2) decides how far into the lane an AI driver will go.

### Phase 4: holding a lane side by side (the spotter)
- When a car overlaps alongside (a "car low" or "car high" call), both AI cars **lock their current lane**
  until the overlap clears or the corner exit is past. Neither returns to the ideal line through it.
- `Awareness` decides how quickly the lock engages and how much margin is left. Low Awareness gives
  late reactions and the occasional squeeze, which is where cautions come from on short tracks.
- Pass commitment lasts for the length of the manoeuvre (until clear or the corner exit), not a fixed
  `commitHoldSeconds`.
- Test it in `PackRaceSimTests`: two-wide through a corner should cause no contact with high Awareness, and
  occasional contact with low Awareness.

### Phase 5: drafting as a decision
- Pull out when the tow speed is enough to clear the car ahead before the next corner's braking point, not
  just because the AI is quicker. Superspeedways also need **pushing and lining up**: a car pulling out
  takes its pusher with it, so pass decisions are made per draft line, not per car.
- Aggression controls how early the AI pulls out and whether it will go three-wide.

### Phase 6: put the remaining stats to use
- `TyreManagement` slows tyre wear (`TireState`). `FuelManagement` reduces fuel burn and widens the
  fuel-save window.
- `Adaptability` sets how quickly a driver gets up to full event strength over the early laps of practice.
- Add a **Finishing** stat, or reuse an existing one, for mechanical reliability. NR2003's experience
  suggests keeping its effect small.

### Phase 7: player-facing sliders
- **AI Strength** and **AI Aggression** settings (NR2003's two sliders) feed `TrackConditions.AiPaceMultiplier`
  and an aggression scale applied after the event roll. Defaults: 100% and the roster's own value.
